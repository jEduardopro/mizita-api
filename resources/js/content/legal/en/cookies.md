# Cookie Policy

A cookie is a small file a site stores in your browser to remember something between one page and the next. Without them, signing in would be impossible: every request would reach the server with no idea who made it.

This policy lists **every** cookie Mizita sets. It is not a sample list — it is the complete one.

## 1. We use no tracking cookies

Worth saying before anything else, because it changes everything that follows.

Mizita **sets no advertising cookies, no analytics cookies, and no third-party cookies**. There are no social network pixels, no external measurement tools, no behavioural profiles. Nothing we store leaves our servers or serves to follow you around other sites.

That is why **you will see no consent banner** when you arrive. The only cookies we use are the ones strictly necessary for the service to work and be secure, and those need no prior consent: without them there is simply no service.

If we ever add a cookie that does need consent, we will update this page and ask your permission **before** setting it, not after.

## 2. The cookies we do use

| Cookie | Source | What it does | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mizita-session` | Mizita | Identifies your open session. It is what lets the server know it is you as you move from page to page. It is encrypted, and the browser lets no script read it. | 2 hours of inactivity |
| `XSRF-TOKEN` | Mizita | Protects forms against request forgery: it travels with every submission to prove it came from one of our pages and not from another site. The application needs to read it, so it is not hidden from scripts. | 2 hours of inactivity |
| `locale` | Mizita | Remembers whether you chose to read the site in Spanish or English. It contains only `es` or `en`; it identifies nobody. | 1 year |
| `remember_web_…` | Mizita | Only if you tick «Keep me signed in» when logging in. Lets us recognise you when you come back without asking for your password again. | Until you sign out |

Session cookies refresh as you browse: the two hours count from your last action, not from when you arrived.

## 3. Nothing is stored if you only look

If you come to Mizita, view a business's page, and leave without booking or signing in, we leave no cookie behind — at most the language one, if you switched it by hand.

## 4. How to turn them off

You can block or delete cookies from your browser's settings. Each one names it differently, but all of them allow it:

- **Chrome** — Settings → Privacy and security → Cookies and other site data
- **Safari** — Settings → Privacy → Manage website data
- **Firefox** — Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data
- **Edge** — Settings → Cookies and site permissions

You can also browse in a private window, which discards them on close.

Bear the consequence in mind: if you block Mizita's cookies **you will not be able to sign in**, and forms will stop submitting because the server will reject the request with the security token missing. Booking as a guest will still work.

## 5. Other technologies

Besides cookies, the application uses the browser's local storage to keep interface preferences and a temporary copy of what you are typing into a form, so it is not lost if you reload. That data stays on your device, is sent to no server, and you can clear it along with the cookies.

We use no web beacons and no browser fingerprinting.

## 6. Changes

If we change the cookies we use, we will update this table and the date shown at the top of the page. Where a change involves a cookie that needs your consent, we will ask for it before setting it.
